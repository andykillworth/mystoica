Symbolic Notes
A — The Uncontained Residue
What is deposited at 12:55 a.m. and cited under a municipal code is the body refusing its architecture, leaving a mark no lease can absorb. The welfare check ninety minutes later is the same refusal, moved inward.
B — The Street That Keeps Returning
A single road receives four separate emergencies in one day — breath, mind, collision, animal — as if East Garden Road were not a location but a wound that keeps reopening at the same coordinates.
C — The Gravy That Was Already Spoken For
A product named for speed — 'Meals Made in Minutes' — conceals a contaminant with an incubation period measured in days, a quiet rebuke to anything that claims to be instant.

Each exhibit is a container failing privately before it fails publicly: the apartment, the street, the clamshell package — all breached from within, all logged only after the fact.

The digit 4 surfaces three separate times — in the citation, in the call count, in the SKU — while 5 answers twice, as if two currents were running under the same day.

Four is the number of a thing that has been sealed and reopened; its repetition across an ordinance citation, an emergency sequence, and a product code suggests containment itself is the recurring theme, not coincidence.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under the Adjacency Clause, no record needs to cite another to be bound to it; proximity in time is itself a form of custody. A citation filed in Wisconsin at 12:55 a.m., a call sequence logged in Pennsylvania across the same calendar date, and a federal alert still active from two weeks prior do not require a shared cause — they require only a shared window of exposure, which the clause treats as sufficient grounds for entanglement.
