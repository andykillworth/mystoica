Three ledgers, filed an ocean of jurisdiction apart, agree on one thing without ever touching: something crossed a line it had no license to cross.

Symbolic Notes
A — The Discharge
A gunshot logged on a street named for a tree is the record of a boundary tested at its root; Cedar Avenue becomes the western marker-post in a chain of perimeter failures.
B — The Encased Crossing
A sausage sealed in a can and stamped with a foreign mark of inspection is contraband disguised as the ordinary — meat that entered the republic without ever being seen to enter.
C — The Rising Floor
A fire that starts beneath a house and climbs toward the people living above it is the oldest symbol there is: what is buried does not stay buried, and foundations are promises that can be broken.

All three exhibits are failures of the same kind — a perimeter, a border, a floor — each one a membrane assumed to hold, each one breached within the same reporting cycle without any agency on either record knowing of the other.

Numerology

No digit repeats across the five reductions — in a custody chain, the absence of an echo is itself read as a closed loop, not an open one.

The unbroken sequence 2, 5, 7, 8, 9 climbs without ever doubling back, which under the Adjacency Clause is read as a single uninterrupted transit — one incident passing custody to the next without a shared signature, the mark of a chain that was never meant to be traced.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that any three public records filed within the same reporting window, regardless of distance or agency, are not independent entries but sequential custody transfers of a single unresolved event. The event does not announce itself; it simply moves — from a can, to a street, to a floor — and the only evidence it leaves is the coincidence of the filing stamp. Today's exhibits satisfy the Clause precisely: a discharge, a breach of import law, and a fire breaking upward through a floor, all three stamped within a seventy-two-hour custody window that no clerk on any of the three desks was authorized to see in full.

The Cascade

Reconstructing the custody trace in the order the event actually traveled, not the order the records were filed:

1

Washington, D.C. — The breach opens on paper first: FSIS admits a product crossed into the country without ever passing through the inspection it was required to pass through — the original, unwitnessed entry.

2

Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Maryland, Texas — The breach disperses across five states at once, each shipment a copy of the same unlicensed crossing, diluted into ordinary pantries where it will sit, undetected, for months.

3

Hughesville, Maryland — Fifteen days later, in one of the five named states, the same pattern resurfaces as fire: something unlicensed to be there — flame in a basement — breaches the floor meant to contain it and rises into the house above.

4

Coos Bay, Oregon — On the very day of the Hughesville fire, the chain reaches its western terminus: a discharge on a street named for a tree closes the perimeter the import alert first opened on the opposite coast.

5

Three desks, three agencies, three jurisdictions file their paperwork within hours of one another, none aware the others exist — and the custody chain completes itself exactly as the Adjacency Clause predicts it must.

The file closes itself; no signature was ever required.
