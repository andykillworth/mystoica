Symbolic Notes
A — The Sunken Boat
A vessel that goes under at its own mooring is not wrecked by storm but by neglect — weight accumulating below the waterline until the thing that was meant to float simply stops floating. It requires an outside hand, Sea Tow's hand, to be raised again.
B — The Ground Cinnamon
Cinnamon is already the color of rust and old blood; it is the one spice built to hide what's been added to it. Lead inside a thing already brown is corruption wearing its own camouflage.
C — The Overlooked Fire
No alarm called this fire in. No neighbor smelled smoke. It took a man driving past with no reason to look up — the fire had already reached the attic before anyone outside it noticed it was burning.

Each exhibit is a failure of early warning: the boat sank in silence, the lead sat undetected in a jar on a shelf, the fire climbed two floors before a passerby's glance intervened. The pattern is not catastrophe but the absence of witness.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no record is an island: any two filings separated by fewer than seventy-two hours and fewer than nine hundred miles share a nervous system, however faint. Here the clause tightens around a single motif — the unwitnessed threshold, the second before discovery. The boat sank before the blotter could record the moment it went under; the lead sat quantified only after someone thought to test it; the fire had already colonized an attic before any human eye confirmed it existed. Mystoica does not allege causation. It alleges proximity, and proximity, held long enough under the clause, behaves exactly like cause.
