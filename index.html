Symbolic Notes
A — The Borrowed Plate
A registration cited for being worn by a body that did not own it — the oldest symbol there is, the mask mistaken for the face. It is the opening wound in every custody chain: someone moving under a name not legally theirs.
B — The Nameless Batch
Two thousand four hundred and fourteen sandwiches travel through break rooms and vending slots with no public notice attached. This is custody without a signature — contamination circulating because no one claimed responsibility for the object long enough to stop it.
C — The Assigned Guardian
A mark meant to protect the voiceless changes hands twice in one document, forward and then back, before the Board is asked to name who may finally speak in its name. Guardianship itself becomes the contested property.

Each exhibit is the same error wearing a different coat: a thing in motion — a plate, a batch, a mark — detached from the party legally entitled to carry it, until a record is finally filed to say so.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under the Adjacency Clause, no record filed within the same rolling window is incidental. A citation in Will County, a recall notice out of two Pacific states, and a federal trademark motion in Alexandria do not share geography, agency, or subject — and that is precisely the condition the Clause requires. Proximity in the filing cycle is itself the chain of custody; the documents do not need to know about each other to be bound by the same error moving through three jurisdictions in three disguises.
