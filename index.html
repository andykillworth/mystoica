Mainstream press excluded

Three ledgers surfaced within the same seventy-two-hour window, each recording a different kind of detachment — a vehicle, a mount, a mark — and each insisting, in its own bureaucratic dialect, that something was meant to hold and did not.

Exhibit A — Filed
Frankfort, Kentucky

At 4:55 a.m., deputies were notified of a stolen vehicle on Allen Way. The same day's log records that officers at the police station on West Second Street took a burglary report, and separately officers took a theft report at Blues on Schenkel Lane, where a caller reported the gas station lease paperwork had been stolen from his house.

State-Journal (Frankfort, KY) — Police, fire blotter (Nov. 24) state-journal.com ↗
Exhibit B — Filed
Washington, D.C. (CPSC)

YY Vertical is recalling about 227 VerticalBase Hangboard Doorway Mount Holders because the mount can come loose from a doorframe during use. The system is a doorframe-mounted support system designed to hold a climbing training hangboard for hanging exercises, and the system includes safety clamps while the hangboard is sold separately. The expansion system can lose pressure over time, causing the recalled doorframe mount to detach during use, posing a risk of serious injury or death from a fall hazard.

U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission www.cpsc.gov ↗
Exhibit C — Filed
Alexandria, Virginia (USPTO/TTAB)

Notice is hereby given that the following party opposes registration of the indicated application, under ESTTA Tracking number ESTTA1583700, filing date 09/09/2026 03:39:15 PM. The opposer information names Caterpillar Inc., a Delaware corporation.

Symbolic Notes
A — The Stolen Vehicle
A car taken from Allen Way before dawn is the body in motion that refuses its assigned parking space — the symbol of a custody that was never truly secured, only parked.
B — The Doorframe Mount
A climbing hold that detaches from its frame is gravity's audit of false confidence — the wall that promised to bear weight and did not disclose the terms.
C — The Opposition Filing
A corporation moving to block a smaller mark from registering is the archetype of the gatekeeper — the entity that polices the threshold itself rather than what crosses it.

Each exhibit names a failure of a fixture meant to hold something in place — a parking spot, a doorframe clamp, a trademark class — and each failure was logged by a different arm of the same custodial apparatus: police, commission, board.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under the Adjacency Clause, no record stands alone: a filing in one jurisdiction leaves a residue in the next, provided the two events share a window of no more than seventy-two hours and at least one verb of detachment. Here the verb is consistent across all three ledgers — taken, detached, opposed — each a synonym for a bond that failed to hold under ordinary load. The Clause does not require the parties to know one another; it only requires that the apparatus recording them — police blotter, federal commission, administrative board — be different enough to prove the pattern isn't a reporting artifact, yet similar enough in form (incident number, tracking number, docket number) to register as the same species of document. When a stolen vehicle, a detached doorframe mount, and an opposed trademark all surface within the same week, the Clause treats this not as coincidence but as a custody chain — the vehicle passed from owner to thief, the mount passed from wall to floor, the mark passed from applicant to contested limbo, and all three passings were witnessed, stamped, and filed by civil servants who will never meet.

The Cascade

Trace the custody chain forward and the five-step cascade resolves itself in order of filing:

1

Frankfort, Kentucky — A vehicle leaves its assigned place on Allen Way before sunrise, unwitnessed, the first detachment of the week.

2

Frankfort, Kentucky — Hours later, lease paperwork for a gas station is reported stolen from a private home — the theft of a document meant to anchor a business to a place, mirroring the theft of a vehicle meant to anchor a person to a route.

3

Washington, D.C. — A doorframe mount on the far side of the continent loses internal pressure and releases its climber mid-hang — the physical version of the same failure: a fixture that could not keep custody of what it held.

4

Alexandria, Virginia — An industrial giant files ESTTA1583700, opposing a smaller mark's registration — the institutional version of the same reflex: seize the threshold before someone else detaches it from you.

5

All three records close the week stamped, numbered, and unconnected on paper — which is precisely the condition the Adjacency Clause requires to call them kin.

The vehicle, the mount, and the mark were never meant to meet — which is exactly how the custody chain prefers to travel.
