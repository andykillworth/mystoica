Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that records filed within the same rolling window — regardless of jurisdiction, agency, or subject — begin to lean on one another the way objects left too close on a shelf eventually touch. A campus emergency, a small border town's overnight sweep, and a federal consumer alert are not, on their face, related documents. But filed within days of one another and read at vespers, when the day's paperwork settles, they behave like three panels of a single triptych: a threshold, a perimeter, and a false skin. The theory does not claim causation. It claims proximity is a kind of custody, and custody leaves marks.
