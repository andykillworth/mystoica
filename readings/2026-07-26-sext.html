Symbolic Notes
A — The Restraint
Unlawful imprisonment is confinement without motion, a body made to occupy a single coordinate against its will. It is the charge that names stillness as violence.
B — The Impact
A pedestrian struck mid-transit is a body caught between two states — walking and arrival — frozen by the dispatcher's own words: 'In Progress.' The call never closes; it is filed still open.
C — The Chandelier
A fixture hung above a room, ungrounded, is elevation without anchorage — beauty waiting on a fault to complete itself. It does not fall until the current finds the missing wire.

Each exhibit is a body or an object caught mid-sentence — held down, struck in transit, or suspended without ground — three separate grammars for the same unfinished condition.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under the Adjacency Clause, any two or more public records filed within the same forty-eight-hour custodial window are presumed to share a hidden chain of physical causation, however many counties or state lines separate them — the paperwork of small offices, we hold, is the nervous system of a much larger and largely unwitnessed body, and a strain registered in one filing cabinet will surface, delayed but legible, in another.

The Cascade
