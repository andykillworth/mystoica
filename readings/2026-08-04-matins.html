Symbolic Notes
A — The Broken Perimeter
A door and a windshield opened in darkness, six hours apart, on the same stretch of road — property is not stolen so much as it is un-declared, quietly reassigned to a custody no clerk signed for.
B — The Unseen Alarm
Carbon monoxide has no color and no smell; the two units and six personnel sent to North Christy were answering not a fire but an absence made audible by a machine.
C — The Undeclared Ingredient
Sesame oil pressed into bulgogi and left off the label is a secret carried inside the body of the product itself — the allergen was never hidden, only unspoken.

Each exhibit is a record of entry without confession: a house, a lung, a body, all crossed by something that left no statement behind — only a number, a time-stamp, and a silence where a name should be.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under the Adjacency Clause, no record stands alone; each blotter, dispatch log, and federal alert is understood as a fragment of one continuous intake ledger, its custody chain scattered across jurisdictions precisely so that no single office could ever recognize the whole of what it was processing. The burglary at Williams Road, the carbon monoxide call on North Christy, and the undeclared sesame in a Jessup meat locker are not separate incidents. They are sequential entries in a single unwitnessed intake — property, breath, and body, each breached and each left administratively silent about the fact.
