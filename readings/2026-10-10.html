Symbolic Notes
A — The Wall That Would Not Show Its Fire
A building not yet finished caught light inside its own framing, invisible from outside, so that the crews had to tear the structure open to find what was already consuming it. It is the oldest symbol there is: the thing built to shelter you is the thing hiding the danger.
B — The Warrant That Waited
A debt filed somewhere else, by some other authority, found a body on a named street corner and collected on it without warning. The warrant did not arrive; it was simply always there, dormant, attached to the man like a second shadow.
C — The Seat Unmade by Instruction
An object built to hold an infant safely is revealed as unstable, and its undoing is ritualized: mark it, dismember it, photograph the wound, mail the evidence. The thing meant to cradle becomes the thing that must be dismantled by the very hands that trusted it.

Each exhibit is a structure that was trusted to hold something — a frame, a body, a child — and each one is found, within hours of the others, to have been failing from the inside all along.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no record is filed in true isolation — that a fire log in one county, a sheriff's blotter in another, and a federal consumer bulletin in a third are not separate ledgers but three windows cut into the same wall, and that whatever moves behind that wall will eventually show itself in all three frames within the same news cycle, this time choosing fire, custody, and the furniture of infancy as its three faces.

The Cascade

Trace the custody of the failure as it moves from structure to body to object, each one surrendering its secret at the exact moment it is forced open.

1

Lompoc, California — A structure not yet finished burns from a seam no one can see, and the first responders must open its walls before the fire will admit to existing.

2

Kewanee, Illinois — A different kind of concealed debt surfaces on North Grove Street — not flame but paperwork, a warrant that has been sitting inside the system the whole time, waiting for a name to walk past it.

3

Across an ocean of manufacture, a bath seat sold under the name ONTHEWEI is found to carry the same defect as the wall and the warrant: a failure built into the object from the date of its making, undetectable until use.

4

The remedy for the seat requires the owner to become the demolition crew — marking, cutting, photographing — performing in miniature what the Lompoc firefighters performed on the building and what the Henry County Jail performed on Meeker.

5

Three unrelated custodians — a fire department, a sheriff's office, a federal safety commission — each complete a chain of custody over something that failed quietly from within, and each closes the file within forty-eight hours of the other two.

The wall, the warrant, and the seat were never filed together, and that is exactly how you know they belong together.
