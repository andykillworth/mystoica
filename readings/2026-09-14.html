Symbolic Notes
A — The Suspended License
A revoked right to move, caught mid-transit on an interstate whose very name promises passage. The state does not stop the body; it stops the permission.
B — The Broken Phones
Two devices shattered on Liberty Street, the address itself a small joke the record does not acknowledge. Contempt here is literal: a court order treated as noise.
C — The Downed Wire
Current escaping its channel into dry brush, an energized line waiting to be respected as a threat rather than admired as a curiosity.

Each exhibit is a conduit failing at the exact point it was built to carry something safely — motion, voice, or power — and each failure was logged by an agency whose only job is to note that the line went down.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under what we've been calling the Adjacency Clause, incidents logged within the same rolling week-window are treated not as coincidence but as a single distributed event, expressed locally through whichever conduit happens to be nearest — a road, a phone, a wire. The clause doesn't require contact between the parties; it requires only that the failures share a calendar. Here, three infrastructures — vehicular, communicative, electrical — failed within seventy-two hours of one another, each recorded by a different keeper of records who never spoke to the other two. That silence between record-keepers is, itself, the proof the clause asks for.

The Cascade

The custody trace does not follow a person. It follows the current.

1

West Sequim Bay Road, WA — The first cut: a power line drops in high wind, arcs once, and lights the dry brush at the shoulder. Fire District 3 logs it among 186 others and moves on.

2

The current, unable to complete its circuit, is understood here to travel not through copper but through the calendar — surfacing three days later, three time zones east, as a different kind of interruption.

3

Latham Interstate Patrol, Albany, NY — The second cut: a driver's legal permission to move is revoked mid-transit. The report calls it 'DMV suspension/revocation.' The clause calls it the same line, still down, now denying motion instead of light.

4

Liberty Street, Batavia, NY — The third cut: two phones are destroyed mid-argument, severing the line meant to carry a voice to whoever the order of protection was written to protect. Liberty Street, having failed to live up to its name, files the report anyway.

5

Three counties, three agencies, three unrelated case numbers — each one certifying, without meaning to, that something built to carry a current stopped carrying it that same week.

The wire, the license, and the phone were never connected. They only went dark in the same order.
