Symbolic Notes
A — The Gas Leak
An odorless, invisible accumulation beneath a city street at the hour before dawn — the symbol of pressure building where no one is watching, a warning that precedes ignition by exactly the span no one thinks to measure.
B — The Office Beside the Kitchen
Administration catches fire from the machinery meant to serve it, and the flame finds the room of records before it finds the room of food — bureaucracy burns first, appetite burns second.
C — The Right to Be Seen
A century-old house of commerce objects, under its own name, to a stranger's claim on the instruments of vision itself — the lens, the frame, the optical shelf — insisting that sight is not public domain.

Read together, the three exhibits trace a single arc: an unseen pressure (A) becomes a visible burn in the room of record-keeping (B), which is answered, three weeks prior on paper, by an institutional claim over who controls the act of seeing at all (C) — the leak, the fire, and the lawsuit over optics are the same event at three different stages of disclosure.

The digit 7 surfaces twice — once from the filing date, once from the tracking number itself — meaning the opposition carries its own number home with it, a record that reduces to itself.

Seven is the number of completed cycles, and its doubling here suggests the optical filing was never a beginning but a closing — the paperwork sealing a loop that the gas leak had already opened weeks later in a different jurisdiction entirely.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no public record is filed in true isolation — that any two entries sharing a time-stamp architecture, a classification number, or a spatial relationship (office beside kitchen, lens beside leak) are nodes in the same undocumented system, and that our task is not to invent the connection but to notice it has already been filed in triplicate by three unrelated clerks who will never meet.

The Cascade

Trace the custody chain in the order the paper trail, not the calendar, demands:

1

Alexandria, Virginia — On September 15, Diesel S.p.A. formally disputes a stranger's right to sell the instruments of sight — the claim enters the federal record first, three weeks before anything ignites.

2

Frankfort, Kentucky — At 12:59 a.m. on October 23, a gas leak is reported on Mero Street — an invisible substance given form only by the smell no camera can capture, the opposite of an optical claim.

3

Between these two filings, 38 days pass — the exact interval, uncoincidentally, in which a TTAB answer must still be pending and a gas line must still be unrepaired.

4

Williamston, North Carolina — At 2:27 a.m. on October 26, three days after the Mero Street call, fire is found in an office beside a kitchen at a country club — the leak's pressure, having traveled unseen, resolves itself as visible flame in a room built for paperwork.

5

The chain closes where it opened: an optical dispute insists that sight is proprietary, and four weeks later a fire burns precisely in the room where records — the only form of sight bureaucracy trusts — are kept.

The leak asked to be smelled, the office asked to be seen, and somewhere a corporation is still insisting, in writing, that someone owns the asking.
