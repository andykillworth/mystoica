Symbolic Notes
A — The Reactive Pair
A bleach solution and an acid cleaner, riding together in the dark of a trunk, cross the membrane that keeps them separate. The vehicle becomes an accidental laboratory; the road becomes the site of confession.
B — The Altercation
Two males on a residential block, drawn into proximity past the point either intended, until the boundary of the property line dissolves into a call for service. Coronado Court becomes a wound seam.
C — The Hidden Magnet
A silicone shell conceals a pair of magnets that want, above all else, to find each other again — through tissue, through packaging, through any wall placed between them. The recall is a warning about longing that doesn't stop at the skin.

Each exhibit is a report on adjacency gone wrong — chemical, human, and magnetic pairs that were separated by design but found their way back together, and in doing so produced the paperwork we now hold.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no record exists in isolation — that any two filings separated by fewer than seventy-two hours and touching on a shared hazard category (chemical, physical, magnetic) are, by the logic of custody, co-defendants in the same incident, however far apart their jurisdictions sit on a map. Under this reading, Pearl City's spill, Clifton's altercation, and PIXLABBY's recall are not three stories but three exhibits filed against a single, larger case: the failure of containment.
