Mainstream press excluded

Three filings surfaced this week, each concerned, in its own idiom, with the difference between what a body is and what it is permitted to say it is.

Exhibit A — Filed
Shelter Island, New York

The Shelter Island Police Department's blotter for Nov. 18, 2026 records that officers responded to a report of a buck stuck in a net in the Heights on the 12th, and Police Officer McEnroe and the Animal Control Officer freed the deer.

Shelter Island Reporter shelterislandreporter.timesreview.com ↗
Exhibit B — Filed
Elgin, Illinois

Imperio Distribution LLC, an Elgin, Ill. establishment, recalled approximately 946 pounds of ready-to-eat beef jerky products that were produced without the benefit of federal inspection and misbranded because of the unauthorized use of another facility's USDA mark of inspection, establishment number 1885, with product distributed to retail locations in Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, and Wisconsin.

Exhibit C — Filed
Alexandria, Virginia

In Opposition No. 91310820, filed Oct. 1, 2026, Fluke Corporation opposes registration of the mark 17B PRO, Serial No. 99644808, filed by Ziboo Industry Inc., with an answer due Nov. 30, 2026.

Symbolic Notes
A — The Net
The buck in the Heights is not a captive animal but a body caught in infrastructure never built for it — snared by a net meant for something else entirely. Its release is not freedom so much as a correction of category.
B — The Borrowed Seal
Jerky wearing establishment number 1885 like a stolen uniform is the oldest impersonation there is: the unqualified body claiming the inspected one's authority. The flesh inside the bag was real. The mark on the bag was a lie told on its behalf.
C — The Disputed Digit
17B PRO is not a creature or a cut of meat but a number trying to become property. Fluke's objection is the instrument-maker's old fear: that precision itself can be counterfeited if the label is loose enough.

A trapped body, a falsely marked body, and a contested number are the same complaint in three registers — something is wearing an identity it has not earned, and someone, somewhere, is formally required to object.

The digit 1 surfaces twice — once in the weight of the falsely marked meat, once in the filing day of the trademark challenge — suggesting a single thread pulled taut between two acts of mislabeling, eleven days apart in jurisdiction but identical in grievance.

Reduced, the five numbers do not converge on a single digit, but they orbit a pattern of ones and near-ones — small, assertive, singular numbers insisting on the fact of individual identity even as each exhibit describes that identity being stolen, mistagged, or contested.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no record is ever truly isolated — that any two filings sharing a calendar week are, by the logic of bureaucratic proximity, in quiet correspondence with one another, regardless of subject, agency, or jurisdiction. Here the clause finds its cleanest proof yet: a deer released from a net it was never meant to enter, meat wearing a seal it was never cleared to wear, and a number — 17B — fighting in federal proceedings for the right to mean only one thing. Each is a body, literal or corporate, asserting that identity cannot be borrowed, imposed, or trapped without consequence. The net, the seal, and the serial number are the same object wearing three different names.

The Cascade

Trace the custody of the claim forward and the chain holds without a single gap.

1

Shelter Island, New York — A buck enters a net not built to hold a deer; its body becomes, briefly, evidence of a boundary error.

2

Elgin, Illinois — A different body — two-ounce bags of beef jerky — is pressed into a boundary error of its own: stamped with establishment number 1885, a seal that was never issued to it.

3

Seven states, Midwest — The falsely sealed body travels outward through Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, and Wisconsin before FSIS surveillance activities catch the discrepancy.

4

Everett, Washington / Alexandria, Virginia — An instrument-maker's objection to a borrowed-sounding number, 17B PRO, is lodged before the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board — the same complaint, filed now in the language of commerce rather than inspection.

5

Shelter Island, New York — The net, empty again, waits in the Heights for the next body that will wander into a shape meant for something else.

The deer was released. The jerky was not. The number is still pending.
