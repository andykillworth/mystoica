Three unrelated filings surfaced inside the same forty-eight-hour reporting window, and each one turns, without exception, on the question of who is allowed to hold a thing that was never theirs to keep.

Symbolic Notes
A — The Contested Conveyance
A scooter or bicycle, its ownership so unclear that witnesses could not even agree on its shape, becomes the first object of the day violent enough to draw blood over possession.
B — The Denied Pallets
A stack of wooden pallets, flatly disclaimed by the man accused of taking them, becomes the ledger-line where ownership is argued not through object but through alibi and location.
C — The Hollow Shelter
A vacant motel, built for no one's permanent custody, catches fire from within its own wall, as if the architecture of temporary lodging could no longer hold its own secret.

Each exhibit is the same architecture wearing a different coat: a borrowed scooter, a borrowed pallet stack, a borrowed room — all objects and structures designed for transience that ignite the moment someone claims or denies them too forcefully.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The standing Adjacency Clause holds that when three unrelated public records surface inside the same reporting window, they are not noise but three faces of a single event straining to surface through the flattening language of bureaucracy; today's filings — a contested vehicle, a disclaimed stack of pallets, and a motel burning from the inside — are read here as one custody dispute refracted across three jurisdictions, three record-keeping systems, and three forms of denial.
