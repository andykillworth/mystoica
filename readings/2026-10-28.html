Symbolic Notes
A — The Feline Exchange
A cat is removed from a wrongful household and restored to its rightful one, logged not as a rescue but as an exchange — property correcting its own ledger. This is the symbol of custody reasserting itself without violence.
B — The Garage on Orion Drive
A structure named for a hunter's constellation burns from the inside, the stored and the flammable consuming their own enclosure. This is the symbol of a boundary failing from within, named by the sky but undone by what it sheltered.
C — The Plowed Field
Fire spreads across open ground until human hands cut a furrow it cannot cross — a boundary made not of walls but of turned earth. This is the symbol of containment improvised at the last possible edge.

Each exhibit is a different technology of custody — paperwork, walls, and soil — tested in the same week and each one holding by a different margin.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no record is isolated from the record filed nearest it in time — that proximity in a filing cabinet or a dispatch log is itself a form of causation, however quiet. Under this reading, the cat does not merely get returned on October 21; it initiates a six-day pressure that must discharge somewhere, and it discharges first through a garage named for a constellation, then through an open field that only a tractor can close. The custody chain is never announced. It is only ever noticed, later, by someone willing to read three unrelated logs as one continuous paragraph.

The Cascade

Traced forward from the surrendered cat, the custody chain runs as follows:

1

Shelter Island, New York — On October 21, a cat is removed from the possession of one party and restored to another — the first transfer of custody, filed and closed.

2

The exchange leaves no further trace in the blotter, but the clause holds that a closed custody event still radiates outward for a fixed interval before it is fully absorbed.

3

Washington County, Texas — Five days later, at 10:45 a.m. on October 26, a garage on Orion Drive — a space built specifically to hold something in custody — fails from within and is found fully involved.

4

Freeville, New York — The following afternoon, at 4:48 p.m. on October 27, open ground outside Freeville ignites and spreads unchecked until Beck Farms arrives with a tractor to plow a containment line by hand.

5

Three different custodial structures — a household, a garage, a field — are each tested within the same six-day window, and each holds only at the very last available boundary.

The cat was never lost. Only the rest of the week was still catching up to where it had already landed.
