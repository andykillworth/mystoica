Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under the Adjacency Clause, no name is ever fully local — a surname appearing as a street in one filing and a defendant in another, within seventy-two hours and under four thousand miles of drift, is legally construed as the same custodial object in two states of matter: absent and present. The wall in Bartonville and the street in Sitka are not coincidence but inventory — the smith's tools, catalogued as missing before the smith himself is catalogued as found.
