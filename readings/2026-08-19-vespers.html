Symbolic Notes
A — The Showroom Fire
An auto dealership is a shrine to controlled combustion — engines built to burn safely inside steel. When the fire escapes the engine and takes the showroom instead, the symbol is inversion: the vessel of containment becomes the site of release.
B — The Restroom Wall
A restroom is the one chamber of a public building designed for privacy without surveillance. A message left there before eight in the morning is a signal sent into the one blind spot of an otherwise fully monitored campus.
C — The Baked Dish
Comfort food — spaghetti, chicken cobbler — is domestic trust made edible. A metal sliver hidden inside it is the intrusion of the industrial into the nourishing, the machine's fragment smuggled past the mother's kitchen.

All three exhibits describe a foreign element breaching a container built on implicit trust — the showroom, the restroom, the casserole dish — and in each case the breach was discovered only after the fact, by inspection or by flame.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no incident is isolated if it shares a news cycle with another incident of structurally identical shape. Here the shape is concealment-then-exposure: something foreign sat inside something trusted, undetected, until routine inspection or unplanned heat forced disclosure. The dealership did not know its wiring; the campus did not know its restroom wall; the kitchen did not know its own casserole. The Clause does not require contact between the three sites — it requires only that the same wound pattern recur across unrelated custodians within the same 24-hour filing window, which it did.
