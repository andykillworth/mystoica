Tonight three unrelated custodies failed at once — a hillside, a doorway, and a tabletop vessel built to hold what it could not hold.

Baker County, Oregon

Baker County Sheriff Travis Ash credits firefighters from multiple agencies with responding quickly to a wildfire reported near Brownlee Reservoir just before 8 p.m. on Thursday, July 23, with containment climbing from 24 percent to 41 percent as crews held the fire's east side from Powder River Road to Daly Creek Road; the BLM's Vale District dispatch lists no confirmed cause, though lightning was widespread in the county that evening.

Exhibit C — Warned
Washington, D.C.

The CPSC issued a Product Safety Warning dated July 16, 2026, urging consumers to stop using at least 53,500 Houswise Tabletop Fire Pits, sold since March 2023, after more than two dozen reports of flame jetting and unexpectedly high flames resulted in seven injuries, including third-degree burns; manufacturer Furmia LLC has not agreed to a recall or any consumer remedy.

A landscape, a household, and a household object each pass into someone else's keeping without a clean handoff — the same shape of failed custody recurring at three different scales.

The digit 7 surfaces three separate times — once from a clock, once from a calendar, once from a body count — without any of the three exhibits referencing the others.

Seven is the number that closes a cycle rather than continuing one, and its triple, independent arrival suggests the night's custodies were all reaching the same kind of ending at once.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The standing Adjacency Clause holds that two records filed within the same news cycle, however distant their geography, enter a provisional custody of one another — not causation, but proximity treated as evidence. Tonight's clause extends further: a wildfire with no named cause, a dog with no present owner, and a fire pit with no accepted recall are three instances of the same unclaimed hazard, each one waiting for a custodian who has not yet stepped forward. The Adjacency Clause does not ask who is responsible. It asks only who is left holding the thing when the responsible party has gone.

The Cascade

Follow the custody trace forward, exhibit to exhibit, and the shape becomes hard to unsee.

1

Baker County, Oregon — Lightning strikes near Brownlee Reservoir and ignites a fire that belongs to no one — the first unclaimed custody of the night.

2

Baker County, Oregon — By Thursday night, Sheriff's crews take formal custody of the fire's edges, converting an authorless ignition into a jurisdictional line held at 41 percent.

3

Federal Way, Washington — Two counties and one state away, a different custody breaks first — a vulnerable adult is arrested on July 10, and a dependent life is left inside a locked unit.

4

Federal Way, Washington — At 1:24 a.m., officers perform the same gesture as the fire crews at 41 percent — they take partial, temporary custody of what was abandoned, and carry it back to the station.

5

The chain does not need a motive to be real; it only needs a shape that keeps repeating.
