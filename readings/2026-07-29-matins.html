Symbolic Notes
A — The Truncated Charge
A citation that stops mid-sentence is not an accident of transcription; it is the record admitting it cannot finish naming what happened at the line. The border, here, is not a wall but an unfinished clause.
B — The Unblessed Cargo
Bacon that crosses a national boundary without reinspection is cargo that has slipped the rite of passage entirely. It travels west anyway, carrying its uninspected status into three more states like a debt no one has called due.
C — The Undetermined Flame
A fire with no traceable origin is the purest form of the unverified threshold: something ignited, something crossed from unlit to lit, and the paperwork simply says cause unknown.

A citation without an ending, a cargo without a stamp, and a fire without a cause are the same event wearing three different jurisdictions — each one a door that opened without anyone confirming what walked through it.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no public record is filed in true isolation — that anything logged within the same rolling week shares a border with everything else logged in that week, whether or not the agencies involved ever compare notes. Today's exhibits test the clause at its most literal: an international boundary, an import boundary, and a containment boundary, each breached in the same seventy-two hours, each leaving its breach undocumented in the exact same place — the reason. The theory does not claim causation. It claims custody: that once a record enters this shared week, it cannot fully leave the others' company.
