Symbolic Notes
A — The Redacted Address
A crime with no coordinates is a wound with no scar tissue; the blot where a location should be is not an absence but a second, quieter kind of presence.
B — The Netted Bat
The bat is the oldest emblem of contamination-without-contact — three days inside a wall before anyone noticed, then caught, examined, and returned to the sky clean.
C — The Bleating Goats
An animal sound repeated past the point of tolerance becomes an alarm system nobody installed; the warning issued to the owner is really a warning issued to the record itself.

Concealment, contamination, and complaint are the same civic reflex wearing three different coats — a redaction, a net, and a warning — each one a small machine for putting disorder back inside a container before dusk.

Eleven surfaces twice — once at night in Wisconsin, once at midmorning in Washington — the same hour standing at opposite ends of a single day like a held door.

Two elevens bracket the record like parentheses, and everything filed between them — the goats, the warning, the release — occurs inside that bracket, as if the day needed a frame before it could hold its own disorder.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under the Adjacency Clause, no incident is an island; every report filed within the same rotation of the planet is presumed to be in custodial contact with every other, however distant the jurisdictions. The clause does not require intention, only proximity in time — and time, unlike geography, cannot be redacted. What moves between Madison and Federal Way and Sitka is not information but pressure: an unspoken threat displaces into a domestic breach, which displaces into an animal complaint, each transfer quieter than the last, each one absorbing what the previous file could not hold.

The Cascade

Trace the custody of the disturbance across its three recorded hand-offs:

1

Madison, Wisconsin — At 11:22 p.m. a threat is made and the location is struck from the public record — the first containment, achieved by omission.

2

Federal Way, Washington — Twelve hours later and two time zones west, something that has been living inside a wall for three days is finally noticed — the omission becomes a body.

3

Federal Way, Washington — The body is caught in a net at 11:17 a.m., the second eleven, closing the bracket the first eleven opened the night before.

4

Federal Way, Washington — The bat is released outdoors, clean, into open air — containment resolved not by force but by verification and return.

5

Sitka, Alaska — By 10:43 a.m. the residue of the day's pressure surfaces as sound rather than substance — goats bleating past the point of tolerance, a warning issued to a owner who is, in this reading, merely the last custodian of an alarm that began as a redacted address.

The record never uses the word contagion, but the custody chain does not need it to.
