Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that when two or more obscure public records surface within the same reporting cycle and share a structural verb — crossed, drifted, entered, passed — the record itself becomes evidence of a single unlogged event refracted through three separate custodial systems. A harbor does not know about a meatpacking plant, and a bank teller does not know about either, but all three institutions exist to certify that a boundary was respected, and on this Saturday all three institutions logged the same boundary being ignored. The dock did not ask permission to leave its slip; the bacon did not wait for a stamp; the fraud did not present identification. What connects them is not conspiracy but constellation — the same rupture, appearing in triplicate because no single ledger was built wide enough to hold it.
