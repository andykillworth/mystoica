Symbolic Notes
A — The Wrapped Ember
A fire folded three times over before it ever touched open air is not an accident so much as a ritual of containment gone slightly wrong — the ember insisting on being seen despite every layer meant to hide it.
B — The Collapsing Tower
A structure built to hold a child upright, recalled because it folds inward on itself, is the domestic version of the same wrapped ember: a shape designed for safety that reveals, under pressure, its own capacity to close around what it was meant to protect.
C — The Silent Alarm
A carbon monoxide detector sounding at 3:20 in the morning with no illness reported is the quietest of the three enclosures — a gas with no odor, a room with no flame, an alarm that exists only to prove the walls held.

Each exhibit is a container tested by an invisible or half-visible force — flame, gravity, gas — and each is caught in the narrow window between failure and record, the moment just before the enclosure would have given way entirely.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no record filed within the same rolling week as another, however distant in jurisdiction, is truly unconnected — that the public ledger itself is a single continuous document artificially divided by county lines. Under this reading, a paper-bag fire in coastal South Carolina and a carbon monoxide call in rural Jefferson County are not two stories but two paragraphs of one entry, separated only by the accident of which desk received them. The CPSC's tower-stool recall, filed nationally and therefore adjacent to everywhere, becomes the clause's proof: it touches every jurisdiction Mystoica reads from, and so it touches these two directly.

The Cascade

Trace the custody of the enclosed object across its three jurisdictions and the chain does not break.

1

Mount Pleasant, South Carolina — A cigarette is folded into paper, folded again into a bag, folded a third time into intent — the fire is wrapped before it is ever released, and still it finds the sidewalk.

2

3

4

Henderson, New York — Four hundred miles north and at the deep end of the night, a Jefferson County detector completes the triad: an enclosure with no visible fire and no visible collapse, only gas, sensed and logged as 'no illness' — the alarm that confirms the room held.
