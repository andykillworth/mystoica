Symbolic Notes
A — The Crossed Threshold
Wolf Point sits where county jurisdiction folds into tribal court, and the blotter itself becomes a hinge — an incident filed in one system but adjudicated in another. It is the record of a body passed from one authority to a second without ever leaving the same block.
B — The Unwitnessed Passage
Twenty-four thousand nine hundred pounds moved through the world with no inspector's mark on it — food that traveled fully formed but uncertified, present in every sense but the one that would have made it official.
C — The Repeating Door
One address, one courthouse, two bodies walked through it fifty minutes apart, the log itself a metronome ticking between disturbance and disturbance, arrest and arrest, the same door opening on the same hour.

Each exhibit is a threshold that failed to register as a threshold — a boundary crossed so cleanly it left only the paperwork behind, never the sensation of having crossed it.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no filing is an island — that a document entered into any public record within the same rolling week as another becomes, however faintly, evidentiary of it. Wolf Point's tribal hinge, Boston's uninspected pounds, and Carthage's fifty-minute door are not separate paperwork; they are the same clause, executed three times in three jurisdictions, each copy slightly out of sync with the others, the way a single order can be delivered to three different desks before anyone notices it was ever one order at all.

2

3

4
