Symbolic Notes
A — The Order
An order of protection is a boundary made of language rather than material, and its violation is always a rupture that predates the arrest by days. The report was filed on the 28th; the custody didn't close the wound until the 30th — a gap where the boundary existed only as a threat.
B — The Threshold
Two crossings, eleven minutes apart: one violent and unauthorized, into a home on Wyoming Avenue; one sanctioned and ceremonial, into a church. The same dispatch desk logs the broken door and the escorted body as though they were the same event wearing two faces.
C — The Ignition
A fire that starts on the south side of a butte and is driven east by wind is a boundary event turned inside out — the containment line is not a wall but a direction, and the direction was decided before anyone arrived to fight it.

Each exhibit is a record of a boundary — legal, domestic, geological — failing to hold and then being re-drawn by force, always after the fact, always downwind of the original breach.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The standing Adjacency Clause holds that any two civic records filed within the same rolling twenty-four-hour window are not coincidental but co-custodial — that the state's paperwork, however scattered across counties and time zones, is a single ledger being written in installments. Under this clause, an order of protection broken in Herkimer County does not simply precede a home invasion three hours west in Jefferson County, New York; it authorizes it, the way a struck match authorizes a flame it will never touch. The Round Butte Fire, filed under its own incident number the same Saturday, is not a separate hazard but the ledger's third installment — the same rupture, translated into wind and grass because paper had run out of room.
