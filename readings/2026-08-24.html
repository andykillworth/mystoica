Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that any three public records filed within the same seventy-two-hour window share a hidden custodial thread, regardless of geography, because the record-keeping apparatus itself — the same wire services, the same federal alert architecture, the same small-paper blotter format — behaves as a single nervous system. Ohio and Illinois are not connected by highway here; they are connected by the fact that both appear, by name, on the same distribution list filed out of Washington. Once two towns share a document, they share a fate, however administrative that fate first appears.
