Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that any two or more public records entered into separate, unrelated systems within the same forty-eight-hour filing window are never merely coincidental — they are fragments of a single event that has been distributed across jurisdictions to avoid detection. Today's filing window produced a wound logged before witnesses woke, a fire discovered only after it had already outgrown its point of origin, and a trademark voided not from the moment of its exposure but from the moment of its birth. Read separately, these are three unrelated Tuesdays. Read under the Clause, they are one incident wearing three municipal disguises, each disguise chosen specifically because the other two systems would never think to cross-reference it.
