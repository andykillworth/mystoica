Symbolic Notes
A — The Bear at the Edge of the Record
A bear crossing into a blotter of theft and ospreys is wildness intruding on the ledger of ordinary offenses. It is the animal that does not know it has been filed.
B — The Hero With a Battery Inside

C — The Cured Meat With the Hidden Ingredient
Jerky, preserved and shelf-stable, still carries an allergen no label confessed. Preservation does not guarantee disclosure; what is dried can still deceive.

Each exhibit is a container that fails to announce its true interior — a county blotter that cannot classify a bear, a hero's helmet wired with a battery it never labeled, a strip of dried meat withholding what it contains.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under the Adjacency Clause, no filing is ever truly isolated; each is a custody transfer of hidden risk from one authority to the next, and today's three exhibits — wildlife, plastic, and protein — were logged into the record within the same rotation of the clock, which is not coincidence but concurrence. The clause holds that when three unrelated bureaus, on the same calendar day, each describe a surface that fails to disclose its interior, the pattern is not administrative. It is confessional.
