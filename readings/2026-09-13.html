Symbolic Notes
A — The Unmarked Vessel
A snack food crossing a closed border with no stamp to say where it came from is the oldest symbol there is: contraband wearing the costume of the everyday. The chip bag becomes a coffin for an unspoken origin.
B — The Unclosed Signal
A noise complaint frozen at 'In Progress' past midnight is a record that refuses its own ending. Someone somewhere is still making the sound, and the county's ledger holds the door open for them.
C — The Borrowed Body
To take a vehicle without the owner's consent is to occupy something built for someone else's motion. At seventy-three, the exhibit reads less like theft and more like a woman testing whose direction she was actually meant to travel in.

A hidden cargo, an unfinished sound, and a borrowed vessel are the same event wearing three different jurisdictions: something enters a system it was never cleared to enter, and the paperwork simply hasn't caught up yet.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

This is the Adjacency Clause doing what it always does — three offices in three time zones filing paperwork on the same underlying event without knowing they share a desk. The chips cross a border without a mark. The noise complaint crosses midnight without a resolution. The vehicle crosses ownership without a signature. In each case the crossing itself is the crime, not the object that crosses. Custody, once broken, doesn't announce itself; it just shows up later as a federal recall, a stalled dispatch ticket, or a name on an arrest log, three signatures on one unwitnessed transfer.

The Cascade

Trace the custody and the same broken seal appears in every hand it passes through.

1

Long Island City, New York — A shipment of chicharrón chips clears a port with no import stamp, carrying an origin it isn't allowed to claim.

2

The absence of a mark becomes, on paper, a Class I hazard — not because of what's inside the bag, but because no one can vouch for where it came from.

3

Utica, New York — Three hundred miles east, a different kind of unmarked event begins: a noise with no listed end time, logged and left open past midnight.

4

Both records now share a status the county never intended to be poetic: 'In Progress' — a crossing that started but was never certified as finished.

5

Huntington Beach, California — By the time the vehicle case is booked on the coast, the pattern has traveled coast to coast: something taken, something crossed, something never given proper consent to move.

Three counties, three kinds of custody, and not one signature among them — the seal was broken long before anyone thought to look for it.
