Symbolic Notes
A — The Stacked Warrant
Five charges gather on a single name like sediment on a single stone — the record of a body trying to outrun its own trace and failing, again and again, on the same page.
B — The Loosed Animal
Containment fails in the opposite direction here: not evidence suppressed, but life released. The horses do not flee the record — they simply leave the enclosure, and are brought back whole.
C — The Contested Name
A single applicant's mark stands exposed on Broadway's ledger, a lone identity now formally opposed by an institution that claims the right to decide what a name may become.

Each exhibit is a boundary tested on the same night — a body against its warrants, an animal against its fence, a name against its owner — and in every case the boundary holds just long enough to be documented before it gives.

The digit 6 surfaces twice — once in the fire address, once in the opposed serial number — a quiet insistence that containment and claim share the same root.

Reduced, the three records give us 5, 6, 3, 6, 1 — a sequence that refuses to resolve into a single digit, which is itself the point: these are records of things that did not close cleanly.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no public record is filed in true isolation — that warrants, dispatch logs, and trademark oppositions entered within the same rotation of the clock form a single latent document, legible only when read together. Tonight's filings obey the clause with unusual clarity: a name trying to slip its warrants, animals trying to slip their fence, a mark trying to slip its opposition — three attempts at release, logged within hours of one another by clerks who will never know they were writing the same sentence.

The Cascade

Trace the custody chain forward, filing to filing, and the shape of tonight's record becomes legible.

1

Troy, Alabama — The warrant sheet opens with Tampering with Physical Evidence — a record of a body attempting to erase its own trace before the trace is even fixed.

2

China, Maine — A thousand miles north, the erasure runs in reverse: not evidence hidden, but animals released — the fence fails, and what escapes is not suppressed but simply let go.

3

In both records the thing that got loose is eventually recovered intact — the warrants stay attached to the name, the horses return to the enclosure.

4

Stamford, Connecticut — The pattern of release-and-recovery resurfaces in the trademark proceeding, where a lone applicant's mark is held, mid-registration, in the same kind of suspended custody.

5

New York, New York — The chain terminates on Broadway, where an institution files to decide, on paper, whether that lone name may keep what it already claimed as its own.

*What is filed does not vanish; it is only ever temporarily unaccounted for.*
