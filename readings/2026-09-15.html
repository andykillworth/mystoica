Joshua S. Stallings, 35, of the 4700 block of King's Mill Drive was charged Sunday with theft of auto (over $1,000), third-degree assault (police or probation officer), third-degree assault (Fire, EMS, rescue squad) and third-degree assault (healthcare provider).

Exhibit B — Filed
Richmond, Virginia

Ukrop's Homestyle Foods, a Richmond, Va., establishment, is recalling approximately 22,880 pounds of baked spaghetti and chicken cobbler products that may be contaminated with foreign material, specifically metal slivers. These items were shipped to retail locations in North Carolina, Virginia, West Virginia, the Department of Defense and online sales, and the problem was discovered after the establishment received a consumer complaint regarding a metal piece found in a fully cooked product.

USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, Recall Notice www.fsis.usda.gov ↗

Symbolic Notes
A — The Threefold Wound
A man strikes the three arms of the rescue apparatus at once — the badge, the ambulance, the clinic — as if the body, once seized, must answer every hand that reaches for it in kind.
B — The Shard in the Communal Dish
A splinter of metal hides inside the food meant to comfort — baked spaghetti, chicken cobbler — proof that even domestic nourishment can carry a foreign edge undetected until it is chewed.
C — The Crossroads Burn
Grass catches at the meeting of two numbered roads, an intersection with no name of its own, consuming what neither road claims.

Body, meal, and field are three vessels of custody, and in the same week each one failed to hold what it was meant to hold — first flesh against its healers, then food against its eaters, then land against its own quiet.

The digit 8 surfaces three separate times — in the assailant's age, in the crossroads' summed numbers, and in the tally of responders sent to the fire.

A triple 8 does not resolve; it loops. Read as the sideways infinity it resembles, it marks a custody chain that does not end so much as fold back on itself, body to meal to field and around again.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no custodial failure is isolated — that when one vessel of care is breached, the breach does not vanish but migrates to the next adjacent vessel of care, seeking a new container. A body that assaults its own rescuers has already exported its wound; that wound does not stay contained in Owensboro. It travels the only way contamination ever travels — through supply chains, through commissary trucks, through retail shelves in three states — until it surfaces as a shard inside a communal meal. And what the meal cannot hold, the land absorbs last, igniting at the exact point where two roads refuse to name their meeting.

The Cascade

Trace the custody chain in five moves, each one passing the same undischarged charge to the next vessel:

1

Owensboro, Kentucky — The chain opens where care is first refused — a man in custody strikes the badge, the gurney, and the clinic in a single Sunday booking, discharging the wound outward instead of absorbing it.

2

Richmond, Virginia — Two days into the same calendar month, the wound resurfaces as matter — a sliver of metal inside a fully cooked communal dish, undetected until a consumer's own body found it.

3

The recall notice widens the custody radius exactly as a discharged wound would — into North Carolina, into West Virginia, into Department of Defense commissaries, into online carts, carried by the same distribution logic that once carried the shard itself.

4

Pampa, Texas — What the body rejected and the meal could not hold finally reaches open ground — grass catching at the seam of Highway 273 and County Road 23, an intersection built only to be passed through, not to hold anything at all.

5

Three units, five personnel — eight responders sent to a field that cannot itself be treated, assaulted, or recalled, only watched until it burns out on its own terms.

The wound was never singular; it was only ever looking for its next unguarded vessel.
