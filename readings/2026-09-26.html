*Three boundaries were tested this week, and none of them held.*

Exhibit A — Filed
Salisbury, North Carolina

A Salisbury Police Department blotter entry for September 25, 2026 records that a bullet fired inside a 2200 block Statesville Boulevard apartment passed through a shared wall into the neighboring unit, where a sleeping resident heard a loud boom and her boyfriend later discovered the hole.

Exhibit B — Filed
Cerritos, California

A federal notice confirms that Sempio Food Services Inc. of Cerritos, California recalled approximately 4,596 pounds of ready-to-eat chicken stew products imported from the Republic of Korea without the benefit of import reinspection.

USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service www.fsis.usda.gov ↗
Exhibit C — Filed
Ashland, Oregon

The Ashland Chronicle's blotter for September 25, 2026 records that 32-year-old James Avery was arrested carrying open warrants from both Medford and Ashland for theft, failure to appear on arson and criminal mischief charges, and was booked into the Jackson County Jail at 2:22 a.m.

The Ashland Chronicle theashlandchronicle.com ↗
Symbolic Notes
A — The Wall
A shared wall is a promise of separateness, and the bullet does not honor promises. What passes through it becomes evidence that the two apartments were never truly apart.
B — The Stew
A product that crosses an ocean without reinspection is a border pretending to have been crossed correctly. The stew is not the danger; the skipped checkpoint is.
C — The Warrants
A body carrying warrants from two cities is a jurisdictional map folded into a single arrest. Avery is not one case; he is the intersection point of several unfinished ones.

Each exhibit names a boundary that failed to do its job — a wall, an inspection line, a county border — and in each case what slipped through was something the boundary existed specifically to stop.

A number that repeats three times in three unrelated records stops being coincidence and starts being a signature; 6 here reads as the number of the breach that was known about beforehand and still not stopped in time.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no record filed within the same reporting cycle is truly unrelated — proximity in time is itself a form of custody. A wall in North Carolina, a shipping manifest out of Korea, and a warrant sheet in southern Oregon were filed within hours of one another, and under the Clause, that synchrony is not decorative. It is the mechanism. The three records were never independent; they were three faces of a single failed boundary, recorded in three different vocabularies — ballistic, agricultural, judicial — because no single agency was built to describe a breach this shapeless.

The Cascade

Trace the custody of the breach forward, and it moves like this:

1

Salisbury, North Carolina — The wall fails first — quietly, at night, with no one watching. A boundary meant to separate two households is proven, retroactively, to have never been solid.

2

Cerritos, California — The failure repeats at scale: a boundary meant to separate the inspected from the uninspected is skipped entirely, and 4,596 pounds of product cross it unregistered.

3

off the coast of the Republic of Korea — The origin point recedes offshore, into a supply chain no blotter or docket was built to follow — the breach becomes untraceable at the moment it becomes largest.

4

Medford, Oregon — A parallel accumulation is underway on paper: warrants pile against a single name across two jurisdictions, each one a small, unenforced boundary of its own.

5

Ashland, Oregon — The warrants finally converge — not stopped by any single wall, checkpoint, or court, but by the ordinary friction of a 2:22 a.m. booking. The breach closes not because it was caught, but because it ran out of room to keep moving.

*What crosses a boundary undetected does not disappear — it simply waits for a smaller, later boundary to stop it by accident.*
