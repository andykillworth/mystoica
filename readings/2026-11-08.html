Case No. 2026.1108
Sourced each morning
Mainstream press excluded

Three records surface today that share nothing but a county line and a question of whose mark may be worn, and for how long, before the body underneath is asked to answer for it.

Exhibit A — Filed
Breezy Point, Minnesota

The Breezy Point Police Department's blotter for Nov. 7, 2026 logs three separate car-versus-deer collisions within the prior ten days — one at 6:54 p.m. near County Road 11 and Cedar Street, one at 6:27 p.m. near County Road 11 and Weavers Point Road, and one at 8:56 p.m. on County Road 11 itself. In the second incident the deer was struck and the deer fled the scene.

Pine and Lakes Echo Journal www.pineandlakes.com ↗
Exhibit B — Filed
Elgin, Illinois

On Oct. 24, 2026, FSIS announced that Imperio Distribution LLC of Elgin, Ill. was recalling approximately 946 pounds of ready-to-eat beef jerky — sold under the Fuego, Original, and Limón labels — because the product was produced without federal inspection and bore establishment number '1885' inside an unauthorized USDA mark of inspection belonging to another facility.

USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service www.fsis.usda.gov ↗
Exhibit C — Filed
Alexandria, Virginia

In Opposition No. 91299827, Inmode Ltd. v. Empower Aesthetics LLC, the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board granted summary judgment on Oct. 26, 2026 dismissing Inmode's challenge to an 'EMPOWER' monogram mark, ruling that the stylized design was so dissimilar from Inmode's registered word marks EMPOWERRF and EMPOWERRF BY INMODE that the dissimilarity was dispositive — even though the monogram was an admitted stylization of the very word 'EMPOWER' embedded in Inmode's marks.

Symbolic Notes
A — The Fled Animal
The deer that is struck and does not stay is the first body to refuse inspection. It carries the collision away from the record before the record can close.
B — The Borrowed Stamp
Establishment 1885 was never earned by the hand that used it. A mark of inspection worn by a body it was not issued to is a small, edible forgery — proof that passing scrutiny and surviving scrutiny are not the same act.
C — The Disowned Word
A monogram admits, in its own legal filing, that it is the word 'EMPOWER' bent into shape — and the Board rules the bending sufficient to sever the claim. The word survives; its ownership of itself does not.

Each exhibit is a body wearing a mark that no longer belongs to it cleanly — the deer that outruns the report, the jerky bag wearing another plant's stamp, the monogram wearing a word the court says it cannot keep. The common motion is escape from authentication, not escape from the thing itself.

Numerology
Three Breezy Point crash timestamps (6:54 p.m., 6:27 p.m., 8:56 p.m.) — each contains a 6→ 6 (repeating)
Borrowed USDA establishment number on the jerky bags (1885)→ 4
Pounds of jerky recalled under that borrowed number (946)→ 1
TTAB Opposition No. 91299827→ 2
Date the Board dismissed the opposition with prejudice (10/26/2026)→ 1

The digit 6 appears in all three logged collision times, and the recall's weight and the dismissal's date both collapse independently to 1 — a pairing the Clause reads as two closures arriving by separate roads at the same number.

Three sixes mark the hour of impact; two independent ones mark the hour of closure. Read together, the sequence runs 6 toward 1 twice over, as if the record insists that whatever flees must still, eventually, be filed.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no public record is filed in true isolation — that bureaucratic language, once loosed into the same week, begins to cross-reference itself whether or not any human hand intended it. Under the Clause, a fleeing deer, a borrowed inspection stamp, and a monogram denied its own word are not three unrelated Sunday curiosities but three stages of a single custody dispute over the right to be verified. The Clause does not ask whether the connection is causal. It asks only whether the documents, read in sequence, behave as if they are.

The Cascade

Trace the custody of the mark across the three filings and a sequence emerges, however unintended its authors meant it to be.

1

Cass and Crow Wing Counties, Minnesota — A deer is struck at 6:27 p.m. on County Road 11, sustains the blow, and leaves before any mark of the encounter can be fixed to it.

2

Elgin, Illinois — Roughly a week prior, a different body — a 2-ounce bag of jerky — is found wearing a mark that was never issued to it: establishment number 1885, borrowed whole from a facility that did the inspecting it did not.

3

The jerky's false mark is discovered not by accident but by routine FSIS surveillance — the same bureaucratic instinct that produces a blotter line, applied to a snack food instead of a road.

4

Alexandria, Virginia — The Board, reviewing a monogram, is asked to decide whether a shape can be split from the word it is made of. It rules that it can — that the stylization is sufficient to end the claim of ownership.

5

By the time the fourth filing closes on Oct. 26, three separate bodies — animal, snack, symbol — have each been asked to answer for a mark, and each has, in its own register, slipped the question.

The deer was never caught. The jerky was recalled, but the body of the claim — who gets to stamp whom — was not resolved, only relocated to a courtroom in Virginia, where it was resolved the same way: in favor of the one who ran.
