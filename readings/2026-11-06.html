Symbolic Notes
A — The Repeating Address
A single block number returns three times in one day's ledger, the same coordinates issuing battery, intoxication, and unlawful touching like a needle caught in a groove. The address is not the scene of three crimes; it is the crime of being an address at all.
B — The Wheel That Will Not Turn
A snack shaped like a tractor wheel, built to suggest forward motion toward growth, instead hardens in the mouth and stops the breath. The symbol here is arrested momentum — an engine that seizes at the exact moment it was meant to roll.
C — The Ellipsis in Dispute
Two parties contest the right to own three dots in a row — not an image, not a wheel, but the idea of a thought trailing off. The opposition is filed from an address that is itself a kind of ellipsis, a single mailbox standing in for tens of thousands of unseen companies.

Each exhibit is a record of stoppage disguised as a record of something else — a street that loops instead of resolving, a wheel that seizes instead of turning, a sentence suspended mid-dot — three bureaucracies independently filing paperwork on the same underlying condition: motion arrested at the threshold of completion.

The number 3 surfaces twice without prompting — once as the reduced street number, once as the literal complaint count — while the two governing dates of Exhibit C and Exhibit B both reduce to 4, bracketing the week like a pair of matched hinges.

A doubled three and a doubled four do not cancel each other; they stack. The threes mark where repetition became visible to the public record — a street, a snack bar — while the fours mark the dates the paperwork was finally allowed to say so out loud.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under the Adjacency Clause, no filing is incidental merely because it sits in a different registry; if two records share a reducible number, a calendar proximity, or a structural rhyme, custody of the underlying event has not yet been assigned, only distributed. The Clause does not require intention. It requires only that the paperwork, once laid side by side, refuse to look like coincidence.

The Cascade

Reconstructing the custody chain in the order the record would insist upon, were it ever asked to explain itself:

1

Ojai, CA — On Oct. 31, Grandview Avenue produces its third filing of the day, the address exhausting the available vocabulary of charges before exhausting the day itself.

2

Berkeley, CA — The following day, a company headquartered three towns north announces that its wheel-shaped product — meant to roll forward — has instead been found to seize in the one place it was never supposed to: the throat.

3

The FDA's publication of the recall two days later carries forward the same count that closed the Grandview file — three — as if the number itself had migrated agencies.

4

Sheridan, WY — Weeks earlier, unnoticed until now, a filing opens at a Sheridan mailbox that serves as the registered address for tens of thousands of anonymous entities, disputing custody of three dots arranged in a row.

5

By the time all three filings sit together in the archive, the number three has stopped functioning as a count and started functioning as a claim — on a street, on a snack, on a punctuation mark — each insisting it arrived there first.

The file does not resolve the dispute between the street, the snack, and the ellipsis; it only notes that three, once repeated across three registries, stops being an accident and starts being a jurisdiction.
