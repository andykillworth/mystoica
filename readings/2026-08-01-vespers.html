Symbolic Notes
A — The E-Bike at the Threshold
A machine caught between categories — not quite bicycle, not quite vehicle — is stopped at a doorway of commerce, instructed in the law, and released back into the dark. It is the day's first sign: motion interrupted just long enough to be recorded.
B — The Concealed Chamber
A firearm already reported stolen is found again inside a body already carrying a controlled substance, and the evidence itself is tampered with before it can testify. Here concealment doubles on concealment.
C — The Sliver in the Pan
The vessel meant to carry warmth to a table sheds a piece of itself into the meal. What nourishes becomes, without malice, what wounds.

Each exhibit describes an object crossing from custodian to consequence — the bike released into record, the weapon released into evidence, the pan released into the body — three thresholds closing in the same twenty-four hours.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no incident report is an island; each is filed within reach of another, and the reach is never accidental. An e-bike corrected in Oklahoma, a firearm doubly concealed in Kentucky, a pan shedding itself into supper in Virginia — three counties with no wire between them, and yet each entry closes with the same gesture: something that was supposed to hold together, briefly did not.

The Cascade

Follow the custody of the evening's objects as they pass from hand to record to shelf.

2

4

Richmond, Virginia — In the same week, a baking pan under routine review surrenders a sliver of itself into a product line — first once, in a batch of Chicken Cobbler, then again, prompting the recall of 22,880 pounds.

The chain closes where it began — with a number stamped and accepted without question: EST. 19979, the mark of inspection, passed forward into refrigerators that do not yet know what they hold.

The custody never breaks; it only changes hands, and every hand today was, for a moment, the wrong one.
