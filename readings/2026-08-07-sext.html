Symbolic Notes
A — The Organ Church
A road named for a pipe organ built into a log meetinghouse before the congregation itself had a name for it — the instrument mattered more than the building, and something was found hidden on that same road two centuries later.
B — The Undeclared Grain
Wheat, present in the package the whole time, invisible until an agency was compelled to say its name out loud.
C — The Middle Settlement
A road that marks the boundary where two tracts of land, and by extension two groups of people, agreed to stop and meet — now the coordinate where five separate emergency services converge on one point.

Each exhibit is a site where concealment gives way to disclosure only under duress — a stop, an alert, a collision — and each is anchored to a place named for the very act of settling or declaring something openly.

A number that repeats across unconnected custody chains is not coincidence under the Clause; it is the fingerprint of a single event wearing three different jurisdictions as clothing.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under the Adjacency Clause, no two public records entered within the same rolling window of days are permitted to be treated as unrelated — proximity in time is itself a form of custody, and once custody is established it survives any distance in geography. Rockwell, Washington, and Kirkland do not share a border, a court, or an agency, but they share a window: Aug. 3 to Aug. 6, the four days in which a road, a wrapper, and an intersection each independently surrendered something they had been holding.

The Cascade

*The trace does not follow substance or blood — it follows disclosure, road name to road name.*

1

Rockwell, North Carolina — At 3:00 p.m. on Aug. 5, a traffic stop on Organ Church Road turns up narcotics — a road named because a hand-built pipe organ once made a log meetinghouse famous before the church itself had settled on a name.

2

The same week, a federal filing dated July 3 admits that a wheat allergen has been riding unlabeled inside a jerky pouch called Street's Beef Jerky Teriyaki Flavor — a second concealment, disclosed only because policy required it.

3

Neither the drugs nor the wheat can be recalled from where they've already traveled; both records close not with retrieval but with an announcement that the thing was there all along.

4

Kirkland, New York — At 7:53 a.m. on Aug. 6, five emergency services converge on Middle Settlement Road — a name marking the exact line where two parcels, and the people on them, once agreed to stop and meet.

5

The chain closes on naming itself: an Organ Church built by German settlers answering, seven hundred miles north, a Middle Settlement Road — settlement calling to settlement across a four-day window.

*The three logs were never filed together, and they were never filed apart, either.*
