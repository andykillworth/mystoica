Symbolic Notes
A — The Shelf Emptied
Four thefts in one town on one night are not theft so much as a slow evacuation of shelves, objects choosing to leave custody before anyone assigns them a price.
B — The Sealed Ration
A soup stamped with its own death date is a small prophecy in cardboard — nourishment that already knows the hour it will stop being safe to hold.
C — The Body Lifted Twice
An address called twice for a body that will not stay upright is a house rehearsing its own emergency, practicing the same fall until someone finally listens.

Each exhibit is a countdown disguised as an incident report — goods leaving shelves, soup approaching its limit, a body failing on schedule — three clocks set to the same alarm.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no incident is filed in isolation — that a theft in Alabama, a recall in Maryland, and a dispatch log in Texas are never merely proximate in time but bound by an invisible custody chain, each document quietly co-signing the others. Today that chain closes on a single date: August 22, 2026, printed on a soup label as an expiration and read here as a deadline the whole file was built around.
