Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no public record is filed in true isolation — that a DUI charge on a residential block, a recalled bottle crossing the Pacific, and a fire log's busiest Tuesday are bound not by causation but by proximity in the invisible ledger that receives all municipal paperwork on the same day. When three unrelated documents surface within a 48-hour filing window, their adjacency in time becomes evidence of a shared undercurrent — call it civic pressure, call it the week exhaling. We do not claim the driver on Monohon Street ever touched a Liizousuda bottle, or that Stoneham's dispatchers know Owensboro's docket. We claim only that the records arrived together, and arrival together is its own kind of custody.
