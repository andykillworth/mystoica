Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under what we have come to call the Adjacency Clause, any three unrelated public records filed within the same rolling week, each concerning a tested boundary, are not coincidental but refracted — one event, splitting across a body, a structure, and a shell because no single ledger was built wide enough to hold it whole. The clause does not require the records to touch. It requires only that each one, read as a boundary report, describe the same failure mode from a different jurisdiction.

The Cascade

The custody trace follows the boundary, not the person, through three separate filings in under seventy-two hours.

The street held by force, the shell held by accident, and the house held by nothing at all — and only the record can tell them apart.
