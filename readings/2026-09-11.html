Mainstream press excluded

Three circles, filed three states apart, each one severed from the axle that was supposed to keep it turning.

Exhibit A — Filed
Salisbury, North Carolina

The Salisbury Post's blotter for Sept. 10, 2026 records that a bicycle larceny reportedly occurred in the 500 block of Jake Alexander Boulevard West around 10:15 a.m. on Sept. 8.

Salisbury Post www.salisburypost.com ↗
Exhibit B — Filed
Aventura, Florida

A federal notice dated Sept. 7, 2026 confirms that Corte Argentino USA LLC, located in Aventura, Fla., recalled approximately 29,628 pounds of raw beef products imported from Argentina without the benefit of import reinspection into the United States. The products bear Argentinian establishment number EST. N° OF. 2025 and shipping mark 26644-AA, and were distributed to distributors and retailers in Florida and Texas.

USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) www.fsis.usda.gov ↗
Exhibit C — Filed
Washington, D.C. (national recall)

A CPSC recall notice confirms that the rolling ball on the recalled Ricky Joy Sour Crush candy bottles can detach, posing a risk of serious injury or death from choking. The bottles, sold at H-E-B, Forman Mill and other candy stores nationwide, contain 1.85 fluid ounces of sour liquid candy applied through a rollerball top.

U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) www.cpsc.gov ↗
Symbolic Notes
A — The Arrested Wheel
A bicycle taken mid-morning is a wheel stopped in the act of turning — motion confiscated at the exact minute it should have been carrying someone forward.
B — The Uncertified Crossing
Meat that crosses a border without its second stamp is a body that moved through a checkpoint no one witnessed — flesh in transit, undocumented, still marked with the year of its own origin.
C — The Rolling Head
A ball built only to roll, and built to detach the moment it is asked to — a small sphere that abandons its socket and lodges instead in the throat.

Wheel, shipment, and rollerball are the same shape repeating at three different scales — a circle that is supposed to stay in contact with its track and instead comes loose from it.

The digit 5 surfaces three separate times — in the theft address, in the recalled bottle count, and in the candy's own fluid measure — a number insisting on being noticed.

Five is the number of the hand and the wheel alike: five fingers to grip a handlebar, five digits reduced from a candy bottle built for a child's grip. The triple 5 reads as the file's own signature, stamped low and repeatedly across otherwise unrelated custody records.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no filing exists in true isolation — that any two records entered into public custody within the same reporting week begin, however faintly, to annotate one another. Here the clause tightens: Aventura's beef is distributed into Florida and Texas retail and foodservice channels, the same regional shelf-space where H-E-B, the very chain named in the Ricky Joy candy recall, operates as anchor grocer. A wheel stolen in Salisbury on the 8th, a shipment mark stamped 26644 crossing into Texas without its second inspection, and a rollerball designed to detach on the same reporting cycle — three circles, filed roughly seventy-two hours apart, each failing at the identical joint where motion is supposed to stay contained.

The Cascade

The custody trace, reconstructed strictly from filing order:

1

Salisbury, North Carolina — 10:15 a.m., Sept. 8 — a wheel is separated from its owner in the 500 block of Jake Alexander Boulevard West, the first circle to leave its intended track.

2

Aventura, Florida — Sept. 7's paperwork catches up two days later — 29,628 pounds of beef, stamped with establishment number 2025, is confirmed to have crossed from Argentina into Florida and Texas without its second, corroborating stamp.

3

Texas retail corridor — The same Florida–Texas distribution lane named in the beef filing is the exact shelf-geography of H-E-B, the chain later named as a point of sale for the Ricky Joy candy bottles.

4

Washington, D.C. — The CPSC's notice surfaces: a rollerball built to move freely inside its bottle is found to detach entirely, the third circle in the sequence to come loose from its socket.

5

Three unrelated custody files, entered within the same reporting week, each terminate at the identical failure — a circle asked to keep turning, and instead let go.

So it reads: not one theft, one recall, one hazard — but one motion, filed three times, coming apart at the same joint each time.
