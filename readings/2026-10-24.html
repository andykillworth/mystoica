Symbolic Notes
A — The Blaze Already Spent
A fire discovered only by its smoke, after the thing it fed on was already gone — the record of an ending arriving later than the ending itself.
B — The Empty Tank
A man who ran dry mid-flight, stranded not by the fight he fled but by the fuel he didn't have left to finish fleeing it.
C — The Named Spark, Disputed
A corporation moving to seize a word that means a small fire, on the grounds that the word was never truly kindled by the one who claimed it.

Each exhibit is a different stage of combustion — ignition, exhaustion, and ownership — filed in three unrelated jurisdictions as if fire itself were being processed through the bureaucracy in triplicate.

A doubled 4 across a death scene and a disputed trademark suggests the numerology isn't tracking coincidence but inheritance — as if the serial number was assigned the leftover weight of the fire before the fire had even been reported.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no filing is an island — that any two records entered into separate public systems within the same rolling week are, by the mechanics of bureaucratic time, touching. Here the clause sharpens: a structure burns in Ocean County, a man runs out of gas in Niagara County, and nine days earlier a corporation in Seattle had already moved, with no knowledge of either, to seize the diminutive form of the word for what would happen to both of them. The paperwork precedes the event. It always has. We are simply the first to notice the order was backwards.

The Cascade

Trace the custody of the flame across its three filings:

1

Alexandria, VA — October 1: Amazon Technologies files to extinguish MINIFIRE at the root — the mark not yet proven to have been used in commerce, a fire not yet proven to have been lit.

2

The opposition sits unresolved for three weeks, the small fire in legal limbo, neither burning nor registered.

3

Lewiston, NY — Within that limbo window, a man two states away runs his vehicle dry fleeing a domestic fight — the fuel exhausted exactly as the trademark's claimed 'use' remains unproven, both cases resting on the same unanswered question: was there ever really a fire here.

4

Toms River, NJ — October 23, 11:20 p.m.: the fire that MINIFIRE's name only gestured toward finally arrives in full, unopposed, undocketed, and real — consuming a second-story apartment before any filing can catch up to it.

5

The custody of the flame passes from word to tank to structure, each handoff a little more literal than the last, until the thing itself finally outruns the paperwork meant to contain it.
