Symbolic Notes
A — The Hinge Removed
A door taken off its hinges is not vandalism but a statement: the boundary between inside and outside has been physically revoked. The blinds stripped from the windows complete the act — nothing left to shield the threshold, nothing left to mark where one space ends and another begins.
B — The Seed Withheld
A seed recalled mid-germination is growth arrested at the exact moment of its becoming. The supplier stays unnamed even as the product travels nationwide — a point of origin kept hidden while its consequence spreads in every direction.
C — The Unit Dispatched
A medical assist call is the body's own threshold failing and being answered. Four personnel, one unit, one block number — the smallest possible unit of civic repair, deployed the instant the boundary of the self requires reinforcement.

Each exhibit marks the same event from a different register: a boundary is breached, the source of the breach goes unnamed or unresisted, and a civic body arrives afterward to re-establish the line that was crossed.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no public record exists in true isolation — that filings separated by geography are nonetheless bound through proximity in time, through shared civic infrastructure, through the invisible lattice of federal and local reporting that catches each event in the same week's net. Here the clause tightens around a single motif: the threshold. A door removed in New York, a seed lot withheld in North Carolina, a unit sent to a numbered block in Texas — three agencies, filed within seventy-two hours of one another, each documenting the same instant a boundary gave way and was, in its own bureaucratic register, restored.
