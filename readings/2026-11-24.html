Each exhibit is a custody case disguised as something else: an animal custody no one filed for, a fire's custody of itself revoked, and a word — guardian — whose own guardianship is in dispute.

A doubled four is a structural number, the number of the stable thing that failed to stay stable: four walls, four burns, four corners of a pen a skunk walked out of unseen.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under what we have come to call the Adjacency Clause, no record is orphaned if it is filed within the same cycle as another record describing an escape from containment. The Clause does not require intention, only proximity: a blotter, a federal safety notice, and a trademark docket need share nothing but their week to be read as a single ledger. What the Clause asks us to notice this cycle is that 'guardian' is the word under strain in all three filings, spoken or not — the police are guardians of the perimeter the skunk crossed, the recall exists because a fire pit failed in its one guardian duty of holding flame, and the TTAB dispute is, verbatim, a fight over who gets to be called Guardian at all.

The Cascade

Trace the custody of the word backward through the five days that bind these filings, and the chain holds.

1

Seymour, Connecticut — An animal is reported, a unit is dispatched, and the thing guarded against is already gone by the time the guard arrives — the first breach of custody, unwitnessed.

2

Washington, D.C. — Eleven days earlier, in the same federal apparatus that logs such breaches nationally, the CPSC records that a different kind of containment — a steel fire ring — has already failed four times over, fuel loosed from its vessel exactly as the skunk was loosed from its yard.

3

The count of the failure, 265,000 units wide, reduces by digit to the same 4 that counts the injuries — the arithmetic insisting that scale and harm are, this week, the same number wearing two faces.

4

Alexandria, Virginia — On Oct. 5, 2026, before either the fire or the skunk is logged, a petition is filed asking the Board to decide who was ever entitled to call herself a guardian of anything — the word itself taken into custody by lawyers.

5

By Nov. 23 the three records sit adjacent in the week's files, none citing the others, each nonetheless a ruling on the same question: what happens to the thing that was supposed to be held, and wasn't.

The skunk left no forwarding address, the fire left its ring, and the guardian left her own name in dispute — custody, this week, belongs to no one.
