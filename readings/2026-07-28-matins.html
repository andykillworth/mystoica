Symbolic Notes
A — The Broken Window
A vehicle's shell, once sealed, becomes provisional the moment a stranger decides otherwise. It is the archetype of trespass — the small violation that precedes the large one.
B — The Arrow That Missed
An aimed thing, rehearsed a thousand times, departs its intended course in front of an audience gathered specifically to watch it succeed. Control is a performance until it isn't.
C — The Merging Blaze
Two fires with two names cease to be separate the instant the wind decides they are one. What was distinguishable becomes, permanently, a single consuming fact.

Each exhibit records the same event at a different scale: a boundary — of metal, of flesh, of flame — that was supposed to hold and did not, and once breached, cannot be un-breached.

Where 2 repeats, the record is describing a pair that refused to stay apart: a body and a vehicle, two fires, two performers standing closer than the act required.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no incident is filed in true isolation — that any two records entered into the public ledger within the same reporting window inherit a quiet custodial bond, regardless of the miles between the county courthouse and the fairground and the burn scar. The clause does not require intention. It requires only proximity in the archive, which behaves, over time, exactly like proximity in the world.

The Cascade

Follow the custody chain as the record itself would follow it, forward, without apology.

The custody chain does not end; it only changes which boundary it is currently dissolving.
