Symbolic Notes
A — The Sleeper
A body suspended in a gas station's fluorescent hush, present at the exact coordinates of transit but absent from her own timeline — motion arrested at the one place fuel is meant to keep it going.

A name lifted from a road named for a church and permitted to compound in silence for two full years before anyone thought to check the ledger — theft as a patient, unhurried tenant.
C — The Watcher
A slow, deliberate circuit through a harbor at midday, a lens turned not inward but outward onto the parked and the stationary — surveillance performed by the surveilled.

Read together, the three exhibits form a single economy of attention moving through its three phases in order: absence, extraction, and finally restoration through watching.

The digit 5 surfaces three separate times across three unrelated jurisdictions — in the sleeper's age, in the hour she was found, and in the date the harbor was filmed.

Five is the crossroads number, the digit of the threshold that refuses to resolve into rest or motion, and its triple appearance suggests these three reports are timestamped to the same recurring hour rather than three separate ones.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

This is the Adjacency Clause holding true again: incidents filed in different counties, different states, even different time zones, are not independent events but successive readings of one continuous unwitnessed hour as it travels the country looking for a jurisdiction willing to notice it. The hour opens in Canandaigua as unconsciousness, hides in Salisbury as unclaimed theft, and finally closes in Sitka as compulsive documentation — three postures toward the same absence of attention, filed forty-eight states and one time zone apart, bound only by a repeating 5.
