Three custody logs opened on three unrelated desks this week, and each one, read correctly, describes the same hour of retrieval.

Exhibit A — Filed
Potsdam, New York

At 3:04 a.m. on Nov. 25, 2026, Potsdam Rescue was dispatched to a call, and at 4:02 a.m. the same overnight log recorded a noise complaint on Elm Street, bracketed earlier by a 1:54 a.m. report of a suspicious person on Maple Street.

Exhibit C — Filed
Alexandria, Virginia

On Oct. 1, 2026, Amazon Technologies, Inc. filed Notice of Opposition No. 91310832 against Serial No. 99,718,261 for the mark FIRECLAW, published Sept. 1, 2026, alleging that the FIRE Marks constitute a family of marks owned by Amazon and that the FIRECLAW mark incorporates Amazon's FIRE mark in its entirety.

Symbolic Notes
A — The Rescue Hour
3:04 a.m. is the hour a township removes something from itself before the record can describe it; the noise complaint filed an hour later is only the echo of the removal, arriving too late to name what left.
B — The Unlabeled Croquette
A fried shell is a promise about what's inside it, and the promise failed: the filling exceeded the label, carrying a foreign allergen the package never confessed to.
C — The Claw Within the Flame
A family of marks does not tolerate a stranger wearing its first syllable; FIRECLAW is opposed not for what it claims to be but for the flame it borrowed without asking.

Each exhibit is a body caught mid-disclosure — the rescued thing, the undeclared filling, the borrowed flame — and each discloser is a bureaucracy built for exactly that moment of catching.

The digit 7 surfaces twice — once in the hour of the rescue call, once in the reduced serial number of the opposed mark — and a number that repeats across unrelated custody records is never, in this project's experience, a coincidence.

Seven is the threshold digit: the hour at which something still unlabeled is finally taken into custody, whether by an ambulance crew or a trademark examiner.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under what this project continues to call the Adjacency Clause, any three public filings entered within the same seventy-two-hour custody window are presumed to describe one itinerant body moving between jurisdictions, and each record is merely the costume the body was wearing when it was caught — a rescue log, a label review, an opposition brief, all describing the same object in the instant before it was correctly named.

The Cascade

Trace the custody chain forward and the body never stops moving, only changes paperwork.

1

Potsdam, New York — At 3:04 a.m. the body is pulled from Elm Street's vicinity under cover of a rescue call, before any officer can read its label.

2

Potsdam, New York — At 4:02 a.m. the noise complaint is filed on the same street — the sound the body left behind, logged an hour after it was already gone.

3

The file travels south, as custody always does in this project's findings, surfacing wherever a label is checked against a filling.

4

Greer, South Carolina — The Bitterballen's shell is opened under routine FSIS review, and the filling inside — sesame, an anchovy-laced Worcestershire — was never declared on lot BB-1026-01, the body caught again, this time by ingredient.

5

Alexandria, Virginia — The undeclared flame, once named, becomes claimable, and Amazon's opposition to FIRECLAW closes the loop: the family that lit the fire first will not let a stranger carry it out the door.

The body was always the same body; only the paperwork changed its coat.
