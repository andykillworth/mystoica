Case No. 2026.1118
Sourced each morning
Mainstream press excluded

Three filings surfaced within the same rolling week — a man who walked away from a named street, a sauce hiding a fish inside a fish-free promise, and two companies fighting over which one owns the right to a single letter's capitalization — and none of them know the others exist.

Exhibit A — Filed
Woodbridge / Stafford, Virginia

Prince William County police asked the public's help locating Dylan Shane Chapman, a 22-year-old reported missing and endangered after last being seen on foot leaving the 2300 block of Opitz Boulevard around 3 p.m. on Nov. 16, 2026, while the same daily report noted Stafford County sheriff's deputies logging an arrest after an altercation on Eustace Road and a public intoxication arrest at a Warrenton Road hotel.

Exhibit B — Filed
Georgia / South Carolina (Nationwide alert)

On Nov. 6, 2026, FSIS issued a public health alert for frozen, not-ready-to-eat meat and poultry products due to misbranding and undeclared allergens — the products contained undeclared sesame, and a beef product contained Worcestershire sauce with undeclared anchovy — with no recall requested because the products were no longer available for purchase.

USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service www.fsis.usda.gov ↗
Exhibit C — Filed
Alexandria, Virginia (TTAB)

In Opposition No. 91288686, decided Oct. 26, 2026, the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board sustained Unplex Technologies LLP's opposition against UnPlex Fintech Corp's near-identical mark, ruling that the opposer had established prior common-law rights in UNPLEX TECHNOLOGIES and refusing the applicant's registration.

Symbolic Notes
A — The Walking Man
A body leaving its own coordinates on foot is the oldest glyph there is: departure under one's own power, voluntary erasure, the self choosing to become a blank field on a report. Opitz Boulevard does not know it has become a threshold.
B — The Hidden Fish
An anchovy folded into a sauce folded into a product folded into a label that never says so is presence disguised as absence — the opposite failure of the walking man, who is absence disguised as presence. Together they form a single error committed in both directions at once.
C — The Doubled Name
UNPLEX and UnPlex are the same four letters wearing different clothes, and the Board was forced to decide which wearer came first. A trademark dispute over near-twins is custody law for language itself.

What binds the three is the problem of the undeclared: a man undeclared as missing until someone notices, an allergen undeclared until someone eats it, a name undeclared as prior until someone litigates it — three bureaucracies independently discovering that absence of declaration is itself a kind of event.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under the Adjacency Clause, any three public filings surfacing within the same reporting window and sharing a date-proximity of under seventy-two hours are treated as fragments of one suppressed narrative distributed across unrelated custodial systems — police records, federal food-safety alerts, and intellectual-property adjudication — each bureaucracy blind to the other two, each one holding a third of a sentence it doesn't know it's writing. The walking man, the hidden anchovy, and the doubled name are not three stories. They are one story that three different government forms were each built to only partially record.

The Cascade

The custody trace follows the undeclared thing as it migrates from street to sauce to signature.

1

Woodbridge, Virginia — A 22-year-old leaves the 2300 block of Opitz Boulevard on foot at 3 p.m., and for several hours he exists only as an absence with a name attached — a man held in custody by no one.

2

Stafford County, Virginia — The same incident report logs an altercation on Eustace Road and a public intoxication arrest at a Warrenton Road hotel — two bodies in excess where one body is missing, as if the county's ledger is trying to balance itself.

3

Georgia / South Carolina — Ten days earlier, federal inspectors confirm that a Worcestershire sauce has been carrying an undeclared anchovy inside a beef product for an unknown length of time — a presence hidden inside a declared thing, the inversion of a man who is a declared thing gone missing.

4

Alexandria, Virginia — At the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board, two companies calling themselves nearly the same name are told that only one of them may keep it — the Board resolves a doubling the way the street never resolved its absence.

5

The three records close within the same reporting week without referencing one another, which is itself the proof: a system under strain does not announce its strain in one filing, it leaks it across several, each one carrying a fragment too small to be read as a pattern until they are read together.

*The anchovy was never hidden. It was simply filed somewhere the street couldn't see.*
