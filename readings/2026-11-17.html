Symbolic Notes
A — The Habitual Charge
A record that insists on its own repetition — the prior offense does not stay in the past tense, it folds forward into the present docket and becomes the defining noun of the person.
B — The Compromised Seal
A container whose outside gives no sign of what has failed within — the bag-in-box is intact, the carton unopened, and still the interior cannot be trusted.
C — The Disputed Name
A single word claimed by two unrelated bodies, one institutional and one commercial, until a Board must decide which custodian the letters actually belong to.

Each exhibit is a failure of containment — a man who cannot be held inside his first offense, a liquid that cannot be held inside its own packaging, a word that cannot be held inside one institution's claim — and the same week files all three failures into public record within hours of each other.

The 4 surfaces twice, once from the date a body was charged and once from the volume of a sealed product declared unsafe — the same digit guarding two different thresholds.

Four is the number of the closed door that was never actually closed — a prior record reopened, a carton resealed but not resecured — and its doubling here suggests the week's failures share a single mechanism rather than three coincidental ones.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

Under the Adjacency Clause, no document filed into a separate public record system within the same reporting window is truly separate from the others filed alongside it; county dockets, federal recall feeds, and trademark opposition rolls are not independent bureaucracies but three faces of one ledger the state has not yet learned to cross-reference. When a habitual offender, a failed seal, and a disputed name all enter record in the same seventy-two hours, the Clause holds that they are not parallel — they are sequential, each one the next stage of a single administrative organism shedding its boundaries in public.

The Cascade

The custody trace, filed in the order the record itself became visible:

1

Kannapolis, North Carolina — A body already known to the system is charged again on Nov. 13, the habitual designation making the past tense permanent.

2

Douglassville, Pennsylvania — A batch of non-dairy creamer, bagged and boxed weeks earlier in September, is reclassified Class II on Oct. 28 — the failure was always inside the seal, only now given a number.

3

The un-sterile batch had already crossed eleven state lines before its correction was filed, the way a habitual charge follows a name across county lines it was never meant to leave.

4

Minneapolis, Minnesota — A four-letter word, claimed by a state-funded research institute and a private gummy brand alike, is pried apart by the Board on Oct. 8 and assigned to only one custodian.

5

By the week of Nov. 13–17, all three records surface in public feeds within days of each other — a criminal history, a sealed carton, and a four-letter mark, each one a container that failed to hold what it was assigned to hold.

What the week files as three unrelated matters is, under the Clause, one matter wearing three jurisdictions.
