Symbolic Notes
A — The Vault Interior
A jewelry box is the smallest sanctioned vault a household keeps — a private reliquary meant to hold what the owner values most out of public reach. Its emptying without witness marks the interior boundary as porous.
B — The Unseen Breath
A gas leak is a boundary failure made of air itself — pressure escaping a pipe never meant to be watched, discovered only by the nose or the gauge. It is the invisible custody chain, broken without a mark left behind.
C — The Unchecked Threshold
Meat crossing a border without reinspection is a threshold pretending to be closed while remaining open. The product moves in full daylight, yet the checkpoint behind it stays dark.

Each exhibit is a different material passing through a boundary that should have held it — a drawer, a pipe, a customs stamp — and in each case the failure is not the crossing itself but the absence of a witness at the moment it happened.

Working Theory — The Adjacency Clause

The Adjacency Clause holds that no filing is isolated — that any two records entered into the public ledger within the same rotation of the earth are already in a kind of custody with one another, whether or not the clerks who filed them ever spoke. Here the clause tightens: Salem, Illinois and Lisle, Illinois are held in the same state's jurisdiction, meaning the jewelry box and the bacon shipment were, however briefly, subject to the same signature authority. The gas leak in Texas is the outlier that proves the rule — the third point required to turn a coincidence into a triangle, and a triangle, once drawn, cannot be un-drawn.

The Cascade

Trace the custody chain forward, exhibit by exhibit, and the objects begin to move as if pulled by the same string.

1

Salem, Illinois — A jewelry box leaves its drawer at Glenwood Acres sometime in a window of weeks, its exit unlogged and its contents unnamed — the first object to leave custody without a manifest.

2

Lisle, Illinois — In the same state, under the same seal of jurisdiction, a shipment of bacon crosses from Canada into the country without the benefit of import reinspection — a second exit performed in the open, yet equally unwitnessed at the threshold that mattered.

3

Pampa, Texas — At 1:26 in the afternoon, five hundred miles south and west, a gas leak is called in on Zimmers — pressure finding its own exit, proving that the pattern of the day is not theft but escape.

4

Idaho, Oregon, Washington — The recalled bacon's downstream trail runs west along exactly the retreat line a fugitive object would draw — away from the state of origin, toward the coast, toward disappearance.

5

The jewelry box, never recovered; the gas, dispersed into open air; the bacon, pulled back from shelves it had already reached — three closures that arrive too late to complete the custody chain, each ending in the same unrecorded gap between departure and account.

The record does not explain where the missing hour went — only that in every exhibit filed today, something crossed a line meant to hold it, and no one was watching when it did.
